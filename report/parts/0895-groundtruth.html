The ground-truth audit

At a glance · The ground-truth audit

Every chapter derives its ground truth from the corpus rather than from hand annotation, which is the reason to trust it. This part checks whether the derivation is correct. In two chapters it was not: sixteen questions scored as unanswerable were answerable from the text the model was given, and four published rates were too high.

How it was tested

ScopeAll nine chapters, every scorer that decides what the correct answer is
MethodCorpus arithmetic only. No model calls, no API spend
The testStructural: does the supplied text carry an explicit subsection heading naming the topic that was supposedly withheld, or one of three narrow patterns added since? Code in shared/contamination.py
Re-measurementCases excluded, prompts unchanged, so every run replays from the committed fixtures
Cost$0

What held / What did not

  • KEEP — Every direction in Chapter 3. The baselines were inflated, so the gap prompt design accounts for is wider than published. Chapter 9’s corrected baseline has no fabrication left to explain.
  • KEEP — Corpus-derived ground truth as a method. The defect was found by reading the corpus, in an afternoon, at no cost. A hand-annotated set with the same fault would have needed the annotators back.
  • DROP — “Unanswerable by construction” as a phrase. Withholding a section does not withhold a topic. The document decides that, and on real labels it decides against you.
  • DROP — Chapter 5’s range verdict. Plain and capture prompt are level on a corrected denominator.

The one thing to remember

Five times in this project the model was recorded as failing when it was reading the document correctly — the zero-width spaces it was right to ignore, the DEGRADED grade that was the accurate one, twelve questions it was right to answer, two more that its own token probabilities pointed to before any test did, and two more found only by reading the answers. Every one inflated a failure rate the book then reported. Check what your ground truth calls wrong before you trust what it calls right, because a scorer that is mistaken in the pessimistic direction produces publishable findings and no error message.

Where this is weakest

The contamination test is deliberately narrow — an explicit heading, plus three narrow patterns: a dosing line addressed to children by age, a “Use of X With…” heading, and an over-the-counter children under 18 years of age: ask a doctor line — so the excluded set is a floor and the corrected rates are themselves upper bounds. Four of the sixteen were not found by the test at first. Two were found by the model: in Experiment 3.5 they were the two most certain answers in the set. Two were found by reading the answers: Sertraline’s interactions and Omeprazole’s children’s line. The test now catches all four. The Chapter 5 correction is a single run: identical prompts collapse to one cached fixture, so the five-run spread cannot be recomputed without paying for it again.
